⛔ The trim is the test that matters. A spread is an average, so a handful of violent windows can carry all of it while the other 95% contribute nothing — and you cannot identify those windows in advance, so an edge made of them is not a strategy. One pool here measured +1.05pp against a 0.60% round trip and looked tradeable; dropping the largest 5% of moves took it to +0.38pp. Without the trim, this terminal would have reported it as a trade.
⚠ "No edge" is the usual answer and it is the honest one. A screen that always finds something is a screen that is fitting noise. The value of this table is the days it says nothing.

METHOD

Flow is measured as balance delta, not "buys". Under Uniswap v4 the tokens live in a singleton and a swap settles through flash accounting, so the visible transfer may be pool↔router rather than pool↔trader and it changes with whichever router was used. Classifying a transfer as a buy needs a list of every router on mainnet and is wrong the day a new one ships. A balance delta needs none of that.
Price comes from the pool's own events — v3 Swap.sqrtPriceX96, v2 Sync reserves — so it is executed state, not an index quote.
Reads that fail say so. Every cell above has three states: a value, reading, and a named failure. An em-dash under a live label is indistinguishable from a zero, a dead contract or a bug.
