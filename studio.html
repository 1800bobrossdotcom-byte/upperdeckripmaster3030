One hundred cards.
Every one a living lens.

The trading card is the form — a size before it's anything else. Palm, phone, two sides: a front that shows and a back that tells. Sometimes it holds data and powers.

These ones do. A card here is one object in four places: artwork in your folder, a lit 3D lens when you pull it out, a power in the games, and a token that reads the chain and changes what it shows. Not four pictures of a thing — the same thing.

The deck

One hundred cards, handmade by the artist. Thirty-three heroes carry animation and mint as 1/1s; the rest are render-only field lenses, plus Lovebeing — one per wallet, bound to you, non-transferable. Cards never retire and never ash.

The arcade

Wager tokens and cards into one pot. A small rake burns — that part is real and on-chain — and the rest pays the podium. Cards move between players; they are never burned in a game. The prize is the having-done-it.

What a living lens actually means

A SuperRare Liquid Edition can hand a render contract on-chain state and let it become part of the picture — price, trading activity, holder balances. So the card is not a JPEG with a pointer at it. It reads what the token is doing and draws that.

Packs burn tokens permanently, and the lens shows the burn. Hold more and the render acknowledges it, in tiers. The art acknowledges you; it does not pay you. That is the whole anti-casino position stated as a mechanic — same anticipation as the rip, the pull, the reveal, but the tangible prize is the having-done-it.

The studio

Gianni Arone — lovebeing. Rare Pepes on Bitcoin did it first; came up as a Fake Rares artist and found a love and a knack for making digital cards. The throughline is MAD magazine and cereal boxes — a picture that is joke, trophy and currency at once — then the casino and the arcade, the machine that promises you win something tangible, then the auction.

It is all anticipation. The Dadaist turn is that sometimes the thing you win is the experience itself. So the token burns so the art can live.
