WHY IT ISN'T DROWNING IN ROUTERS

Every honest version of this idea dies the same way: Uniswap takes approvals from everyone too. So does every bridge, marketplace and aggregator. A screen that ranks by "who is collecting authorisations" returns infrastructure forever and wastes the analyst it was built for.

These two were flagged in the same window, on the same chain, minutes apart. Both are brand new. Both took a burst of approvals from dozens of wallets. Both had those wallets' tokens leave afterwards. One is worth waking somebody up for and one is a router being used — and the only thing separating them is where the tokens went.

A router's users each receive their own output. A collector's victims all pay one address. Movement is not the signal — concentration is.

That distinction was not a design decision, it was a correction. The first version scored the outflow itself and every router on Base came back 111/111, 92/92, 86/88 — because approving a router and having it move your tokens is a swap. It fired on everything and separated nothing. The regression case is now permanent in the suite: a router with 88 of 90 approvers drained across 71 destinations must score zero. It does.

THE LEDGER

A flag with no follow-up is an opinion. Every candidate that clears the bar is written down once, with its evidence frozen at that block, and every later pass appends what changed. Nothing is ever rewritten — a record you can edit after the fact is not a record.

WHAT IT WILL NOT DO

It never calls anything a scam. Not in the output, not in the data file, not in the API. It emits counts and the reader concludes.

That is not caution, it is the product working. A new protocol launching, an airdrop claim, a token migration and a popular mint all look exactly like a drainer for the first hour of their lives — a migration contract genuinely does move everyone's tokens to one address, by design and with consent, and it scores high here. A feed that publishes accusations is a liability to the desk that buys it. A ranked queue with the working shown is a tool. The test suite asserts the words "scam", "drainer", "malicious", "fraud", "thief", "criminal" and "stolen" cannot appear in any reason string the tool produces.
