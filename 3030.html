WHAT IT IS LOOKING AT

Four things, and the last one is the only one that watches a consequence rather than a posture.

  • BURST — how many separate wallets approved it, and how fast. A protocol accumulates users; a campaign arrives all at once.
  • HISTORY — whether it was taking approvals a day earlier. A router has done this every day for years. This is computed, not looked up on a list, so it cannot be wrong about an address nobody thought to name.
  • SPREAD — how many different tokens. Seventy is router behaviour and scores it down.
  • FUNNEL — where those wallets' tokens went afterwards. A router's users each receive their own output; a collector's victims all pay one address.

That last one had to be learned the hard way. The first version scored the outflow itself — and every router on Base came back 111 of 111, because approving a router and having it move your tokens is a swap. It fired on everything and separated nothing. Movement is not the signal. Concentration is.

WHAT THIS WILL NOT TELL YOU

It never calls anything a scam — not here, not in the data, not in the feed. It reports counts and what they usually mean.

A brand new protocol, an airdrop claim, a token migration and a popular mint all look exactly like a drainer for the first hour of their lives. A migration contract really does move everyone's tokens to one address, by design and with consent, and it scores high here. A high score means find out what this is before you sign it — from a source that is not this page. And a quiet score is not a guarantee: a contract with no history yet has nothing to be unusual about.
