THE 33 · STEP 1 · DISPOSABLE

Press and drag the card. The stock dishes under your thumb and rings when you let go, the plates part as it turns, and the die edge walks its hue. Keep handling it and the sheet advances — a new impression, new registration, new ink.

It is a real material now: paper fibre, ink standing proud of the stock with its halftone dots as beads, a spot varnish on the window against bare trim, and a die edge that is metal — no diffuse at all, and the grating is its reflectance rather than a tint laid over the top. Three sources are drawn from all 211 cards in the deck.

why three, and why those roles
Every card here is a composition made out of the deck, printed four times with the plates slightly wrong. The three sources are the picture; the seed decides the masks, the registration and the stock. Choosing a plate is a physical act — the press takes it and the sheet prints again, so the card draws itself back on.
set from committed outlines — no font ships and none is named. 68 glyphs: A–Z, 0–9 and punctuation; anything else is quietly dropped.
why the border changes
A printer sets a rule from repeated pieces of TYPE — border sorts, out of the same fount as the name. Rarity picks the family, the seed picks the frame, so no two cards share a border and you can read the tier off the edge.

THE BUILD · WHAT KIND OF CARD THIS IS

how far the four elements — ground, mid, figure, type — sit apart through the card's thickness. At 0 they are coplanar and the card is a flat print; turn it up and the stack travels through itself as the press runs. This is depth in the BUILD, not an animation: it is there when the card is still.
eight named press failures — the plates landing together, the roller starving, the sheet slurring under the blanket. Each is a real thing a press does, which is why the card reads as a print rather than as an effect. Leave it on seeded and the card picks its own.
the composition is set in THE FORGE above — three deck cards in role order. Ground is zoomed to a field, mid is mirrored at another scale, figure sits near card size and is the subject. Three pictures of the same size average into one; the roles are what make it a collage instead of a blend.
⛔ this is the wrong kind of misregistration, on purpose. A press shifts each plate by ONE CONSTANT VECTOR — the paper went through askew. Radial error grows from the centre outward, which is a LENS artefact, not a printing one. It is exposed because the acceptance test needs a control that fails; leave it at 0 for a card that is honestly printed.
the other two market dials. Price drives the foil die edge and depth drives how far the stack travels. Each card wears them differently — the temperament is part of its seed, so the same reading lands differently on every card.
the environment the metal reflects. ⛔ the artwork never sees it — only the foil does. An environment on the art plate lands as a milky ambient wash, which is a measured failure this renderer is built to avoid. Switch it off and the foil should die while the print stays byte-identical.

THE PRESS · HOW BADLY IT PRINTED

reading the chain…
maxTotalSupply − totalSupply, read over a public RPC. more burned ⇒ more damaged — the screen coarsens, the roller starves, the stock opens. read-only, never a wallet; blocked, the slider is yours.
take it to 0 and the card prints in perfect register. that is the control the acceptance test uses — the offsets have to be one constant vector per plate, not a radius.
a GIF is one full press cycle, looping — the same card, the same seed, every time. once it lands, right-click it (or long-press) and save; the button is there for people who look for a button.
move the light, not the card. a material answers a light — the coated window flashes, the bare trim does not, and the foil edge walks its hue. Shading painted onto a texture cannot do any of that.
a pull is one impression. nothing here runs on a clock — left alone the card is a card on a table.
